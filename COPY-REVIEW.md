### F2 — The headline is the product's name, not the reader's benefit — *Critical* — R1 (Gary Simon #1)

`index.html:101`, rendered as the `<h1>`:

```
AI
Asure Intelligence
```

This is the single failure mode Gary Simon names explicitly: putting your **name** (or job title, or
category) in the headline, because both are *features* — they say nothing about what changes for the
person reading. `AI` is a category. `Asure Intelligence` is a brand name. Neither answers "what do I
get?"

The good news: **the benefit already exists**, one line lower, in the lede (`index.html:102–103`) —
*"Less time lost to the mechanical, more spent on the work that needs judgement."* That is a real
benefit sentence, and it is buried under the name.

**Rewrite (promote the lede, demote the name):**

```html
<div class="eyebrow-rule">Asure Design Studio · Asure Intelligence</div>
<h1 class="d1">Less time lost to the mechanical.<br>
  <span class="g-primary">More spent on the work that needs judgement.</span></h1>
<p class="lede">52 tools built inside the studio, for the studio — against real projects,
  on our own hardware.</p>
```

Benefit in the H1, identity in the eyebrow, proof in the lede. This also fixes the front-loading
problem (R13): today the first two words a scanner reads are `AI Asure`.

`assets/js/home.js:213` renders the expanded card body as `(p.description || p.tagline)`. **28 of the
52 projects have an empty `description`** (P25–P52 inclusive), so clicking the disclosure arrow shows

> `One agent you talk to; it delegates the rest to specialists in the background.`

An affordance that promises more and delivers the same words is a broken promise, and it is broken

Do not ship (1) half-done and (2) not at all; that is the current state.

### F5 — `BOM` used where `BOQ` is meant, with the wrong expansion — *Major* — R12, R19 (NN/g *Technical Jargon — AAA: Always Avoid Acronyms*; Gary Simon #3)

`content/architecture-boq-template.json:10` → `projects_data.js:559`, the tagline for P21:

> `A BIM-driven template for generating quantity-based Building Operations and Maintenance (BOM) documents.`

Three errors in one line, on a page aimed at construction professionals who will spot all three:
1. **BOQ** = Bill of Quantities. The tool is literally named "Architecture **BOQ** Template", and its
   own description two lines later says it produces `structured BOQ lines`.
2. **BOM** = Bill of Materials, a different document.
3. **"Building Operations and Maintenance"** is not the expansion of either acronym.

Adjacent, same class of error — `projects_data.js:480` (P17 AsureBimQc):

> `…enabling users to authenticate with Autodesk's Account Center (ACC).`

**ACC** is Autodesk **Construction Cloud**. "Account Center" is not a product.

This is the cheapest, highest-credibility-per-character fix on the site. To an architect or QS,
one wrong acronym expansion undoes the trust the other 51 entries earned.

**Rewrite:**
- P21 tagline → `A BIM-driven template that turns a Revit model into structured Bill of Quantities (BOQ) lines.`
- P21 description → replace both `building operation and maintenance (BOM)` occurrences with `BOQ`.
- P17 description → `…authenticate against Autodesk Construction Cloud (ACC).`

### F6 — Every CMS tool page shares one generic social preview — *Major* — R18 (NN/g *Microcontent: How to Write Headlines, Page Titles, and Subject Lines*)

`tool.js:348` correctly sets `document.title` per tool. It never touches the meta tags. So all ~51
CMS-rendered tool pages ship the template defaults from `tool.html:7–9`:

| Loc | Verbatim |
|---|---|
| `tool.html:7` | `<meta name="description" content="AI Research & Innovation — tool detail.">` |
| `tool.html:8` | `<meta property="og:title" content="A tool — AI Research &amp; Innovation">` |
| `tool.html:9` | `<meta property="og:description" content="A tool from Asure Design Studio's AI research showcase.">` |

Paste any tool page link into Slack, WhatsApp, LinkedIn or a Google result and it reads **"A tool"**.
This is the version of the page most people will see, and it is the weakest copy on the site — a link
sharing this showcase is exactly how it is meant to travel.

**Rewrite.** Extend `tool.js:348` to set all four from the record:

```js
document.title = p.code + ' · ' + p.name + ' — Asure Intelligence';
setMeta('name', 'description', p.tagline);
setMeta('property', 'og:title', p.name + ' — Asure Intelligence');
setMeta('property', 'og:description', p.tagline);
```

The taglines are already written, already one-line, and already benefit-shaped. They just aren't
being used where it matters most.

### F10 — The empty state uses a word that appears nowhere else — *Minor* — R9, R6 (Lesson 16 "Empty states"; NN/g *Error-Message Guidelines* — language familiar to users)

`index.html:168–169`, confirmed rendered with `Showing 0 of 52` and zero rows:

> `Nothing matches that scope`
> `Clear the filter or widen the search.`

The **structure is right** — it names the situation and gives the next action, which is more than
most empty states manage. The problem is one word: **`scope`**. The controls are labelled
`Browse by kind`, `Showing`, and `Search by name, type or technology…`. The word "scope" appears

> `Nothing matches that search`

Second line becomes an action, not a description of two actions.

**Related, same surface:** when 0 results are showing, the category rail still reads
`Pipeline 10 · Connector 7 · Deck 11`, because those counts ignore the search box. A user seeing
`Showing 0 of 52` beside `Deck 11` has to work out which number to believe. Either grey out the rail
counts or recompute them against the active query.

## 3. What's already right

Not padding. These are things the site does that most don't, and they should survive any rewrite.

1. **It refuses to invent numbers.** `assets/js/tool.js:108–113` renders `Not measured` /
   `no before-and-after recorded` plus *"it is left out of the studio total rather than estimated
   into it"* for the 47 of 52 tools with no timing data. The titleblock states `5 of 52 measured`
   in the footer. An `About the hours figure` disclosure sits beside the `14 hrs/wk` stat
   (`index.html:121`). This is a **credit** under C10, and it is the site's strongest copy decision.
2. **Card link labels are textbook.** `Open ADS Phoenix — L1 Self-Certification →`
   (`home.js:214`) is specific, substantial and succinct — it reads correctly out of context and
   passes NN/g's 4Ss cleanly. Compare the `Learn more` that most catalogues ship.
3. **The kind definitions do real work.** `A button inside Revit, Rhino or Navisworks.` /
   `Files in, results out. No judgement calls.` / `Chooses its own steps to reach a goal. Rare.`
   Eight taxonomy terms explained in one line each, in plain language, with an honest "Rare."
   This is the best writing on the site.
4. **The roadmap gates are falsifiable.** `At least 70% of the 72 mapped ways used once per team;
   first ten agents running.` And Y2 is labelled **`Held back`** with `Deliberately undecided`.
   A roadmap that admits what it hasn't decided is rarer than it should be.
5. **`h10.html:143–145` explains its own existence** — *"H10 has its own page because a client reads
   it directly, so it carries the project rather than the tool."* Copy that tells you why the page
   is shaped the way it is.
6. **Numerals, throughout the chrome.** `52 in catalogue`, `22 model-health checks`, `5 quality
   gates`, `4h → 0.5h`. R16 is followed everywhere except the three cases in F12.
7. **Heading hierarchy is clean.** One `<h1>` per page, descriptive `<h2>`/`<h3>` throughout
   (`What it changes.` / `Inside the tool.` / `How it was built.`). Reading only the headings gives
   you the page. That is the layer-cake pattern working as intended.
8. **Mobile copy is not a truncated variant.** Every body string at 390px is byte-identical to
   1440px. Only the nav labels diverge (F1).
9. **A `Skip to tools` skip-link** (`index.html:35`) and `aria-label`s on every icon-only control.
10. **The empty state exists at all**, and points somewhere. F10 is a one-word fix on a structure
    that is already correct.

## 4. The three questions, answered

### Does the site lead with BENEFITS or with FEATURES?

**Mixed, and it leads with a feature exactly where it matters most.**

- **The H1 is a feature** — `AI` / `Asure Intelligence` is a category plus a name (F2). Gary Simon's
  named worst case.
- **The lede is a benefit**, and a good one: *"Less time lost to the mechanical, more spent on the
  work that needs judgement."* The material is there; it is one line too low.
- **The stat band is benefit-shaped**: `Hours saved each week` is an outcome, not a spec.
- **The taglines are strongly benefit-led**, and this is the site's real strength:
  `Hold a key, speak, release — the words land in whatever you were typing in.` ·
  `Watch a video once; keep its value without re-watching.` ·
  `Drag a slider in the browser; the Rhino model updates in about 200 ms.`
  Every one of these leads with what the reader gets.
- **The exception is P11–P23**, which lead with *what the thing is* — `AI Team Hub is a web-based
  application…`, `The TEP Dashboard is a web-based tool…` — i.e. feature-first, name-first (F4).
- **The `tech` chips** (`Revit · C# · .NET · Supabase · pyRevit`) are features, correctly placed
  secondary, in a sidebar rail. That is exactly right.

**Verdict:** benefit-led at the sentence level, feature-led at the headline level. Fixing F2 and F4
converts this from mixed to strongly benefit-led without writing anything new — the benefit copy
already exists, in the wrong place or the wrong register.

### Is there ONE clear goal per page?

**Landing page — yes, mostly.** The goal is *browse the catalogue*. `See the work` is the primary
CTA, the catalogue is the largest section, and the counting motif (`52 in catalogue`, `52 entries`,
`all 52`) reinforces it. Two things blur it: the hero offers a second CTA (`The roadmap`) that
duplicates a nav item, and the terminal goal — *get in touch* — has a heading (`Talk to the studio.`)
but no button anywhere, and no mobile nav entry (F14).

**`tool_template.html` — yes.** `See it running` is the single action; everything else supports it.
This page has the clearest goal structure on the site.

**`h10.html` — yes, but the goal isn't reachable.** `Open the dashboard` is unambiguous; it opens a
placeholder (F7).

**`tool.html?id=…` — no.** The CMS tool page has **no call to action at all**. A visitor reaches the
bottom of a well-told story about ADS Phoenix and the only links are `Back to top ↑`, `← All
projects` and `All tools`. Nothing says *see it*, *ask about it*, or *use it*. Compare
`tool_template.html`, which has `See it running` — the template that the CMS page is supposedly
built from carries a CTA the real page dropped. **This is the largest goal-structure gap on the
site, and it affects ~51 pages.** Add a primary action to the CMS hero, sourced from the record
(demo link, gallery anchor, or `Ask us about this tool`).

### Are terms CONSISTENT across pages?

**No. This is the site's weakest dimension** and the reason the roll-up sits at C rather than B.
