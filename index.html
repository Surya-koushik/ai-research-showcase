What we’re pursuing, and what comes next

The gates ahead.

Not a calendar. Each gate is a check that either passes or it doesn’t — this is the order we are pursuing them in, and where the line below sits is where we are right now.

  1. Gate 1 Now

    Foundation

    Standards pack approved, meeting capture live, AI policy signed, and two pilots measured against real baselines.

    Gate

    Flagship BIM pilot run against P285 Tower 3 GFC + BOQ — BOQ drafter and Uniclass matcher in assisted mode.

  2. Gate 2

    Workflows

    The mapped ways of working move from documented to actually used, with context files live across teams and the first small agents appearing.

    Gate

    At least 70% of the 72 mapped ways used once per team; first ten agents running.

  3. Gate 3

    Agents

    Agents carry daily production work with a named human verifier on anything generative, and confidential work routes to models running on our own hardware.

    Gate

    Five or more agents in daily use, first-pass acceptance at 60% or better, spend inside budget, local route live.

  4. Gate 4

    The Loop

    The studio can ask its own knowledge a question and get an answer with its source attached — and the numbers come back to the people steering.

    Gate

    Principal dashboards live, “Ask ADS” v1 on the knowledge server, KPI review against goals.

  5. Later

    Year-two decisions

    Deliberately undecided. A fine-tuned studio model, surrogate simulation and productisation are all go/no-go calls that need a year of evidence first.

    Held back

    Nothing here is committed — each needs the first year’s corpus and a hardware check before it is even scoped.

Questions we get asked.

The ones that come up most when someone sees this for the first time.

What is Asure Intelligence, in one sentence?

Software the studio built for itself, so that architects spend less time on the mechanical parts of delivery and more on the parts that need judgement. It is not a product we sell and not a platform we bought — it is tooling that lives inside the way we already work.

Are these real, or are they demos?

Both, and the site says which is which. Every tool carries a status: most of the catalogue is live, meaning it runs on real projects. The rest is in build or still being explored. Nothing here is a mock-up made for this page. The counts on the tools section are read from the catalogue itself, so they stay right as it grows.

How do you know it actually saves time?

Mostly we don't yet, and we would rather say so. We timed the work before and after on 5 tools. Those are the only ones showing hours, and they are still marked as draft figures until a second round of measurement confirms them. Every other tool is left without a number rather than estimated, because an estimate here would just be a guess wearing a decimal point.

Does client or project data go to the cloud?

Confidential data going to a cloud model is on the explicit do-not-do list in the roadmap, alongside AI-sealed documents and AI-made fee commitments. Work that touches confidential material is routed to models running on our own hardware. The full written policy is being finalised — ask us directly for the current version rather than relying on this paragraph.

Does the AI make design decisions?

No. The tools that run without a human in the loop are the deterministic, rule-checkable ones — model-health checks, layer standards, sheet sets, quantity extraction. Anything generative has a named person who verifies it, and code compliance and fee judgement stay with a human permanently, by design rather than by accident.

Can we use these tools?

To be confirmed. These were built for the studio's own delivery, and we have not decided what, if anything, is offered outside it. Talk to us if there is something here you want.

Where does this go next?

Four quarters, each with a gate that either passes or does not: foundation, then workflows, then agents, then the loop where the studio can ask its own knowledge a question and get an answer with its source attached. Year-two items — a fine-tuned studio model, surrogate simulation — are deliberately undecided. See the roadmap.
